## §4.5 Verifier-induced identifiability: bounded-DSL results

We tested an AWM-style verifier-first search (LegC DSL; T0 identity, T1 atoms, T2 composition) on public eval120. The depth-2 engine accepted **0/120** programs: unique coverage 0%, attempt-1 precision undefined, certificate fraction 120/120 impossibility, 334.7 CPU-min total (2.79 CPU-min/task; 56 workers). F1a is vacuous (nothing accepted to re-verify); F1b is INCONCLUSIVE (no unique classes); F2 **FAILS** (hybrid = TTT top-2, 20.0% on the exploratory 20-task subset vs 0% verifier-only; H−T=0); F3 **FAILS at measured depth 2** (acceptance flat for all 120; depth 3 not measured and not imputed). Learned-rule families (0/120), DeepSeek-chat (0/20), and DeepSeek-reasoner (0/10; all completions token-truncated → infrastructure null) give nulls. The n=12 training control yields **1/12 unique solve (0520fde7)**, confirming the engine can certify reachable programs; it is small and not used for a rate claim. GPU-scale T6/T7 checks (100M instances: 0 violations at 1e-6; 2M-task allocation: gap = mean δ, waterfilling > uniform) validate the implementations, not ARC performance.

## §5.4 Limitations and honesty

Verifier numbers are public-eval diagnostics; labels are used only for scoring, never inside the search. The engine is a research module, not a submitted competition entry. An empty acceptance set is an impossibility certificate for the bounded DSL/parameter class, not a proof over all programs. Depth-2 results cannot be extrapolated to depth 3; depth-3 and any 56-core training run are reported separately when available. LLM nulls are compute-limited (token-cap truncation), hence infrastructure nulls, not capability evidence. No hidden-test or state-of-the-art claim is made.
