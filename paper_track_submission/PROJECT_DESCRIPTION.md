## Abstract
ARC-AGI-2 gives each test input exactly two attempts, scored by exact match, in an offline ~12-hour Kaggle rerun. We report a lean, MIT-0 4B open-weight test-time-training (TTT) pipeline that produced **six scored Kaggle submissions**: **S5** (ref **57681632**) **16.25%** (champion), S6 15.42%, S5_ens 15.42%, S1 14.17%, S5+LegC 14.17%, CPU probe 0.00%; the same pipeline measures **20.0%** task-level on a 20-task public-eval subset, and no score exceeds 16.25. A reference public-notebook replication reached only 3/20 on dev20, far below the ~30 notebook band; an earlier rerun errored on a rerun-only budget parse — plus a pre-registered study of the two-attempt decision. On real pools score ordering saturates recoverable coverage, so reranking adds nothing; we report a bounded negative result and locate the bottleneck in generation/coverage. A bounded-DSL verifier accepted **0/120** eval tasks (impossibility certificates) but certified **1/12** train tasks: verifier-induced identifiability is real yet coverage-limited (Theorem 8). No state-of-the-art claim is made.

## Method: one offline TTT notebook
One MIT-0 Kaggle notebook, no internet/API at rerun. Per task: load Qwen3-4B ARC-SFT (7.27 GB bf16); augment demonstrations with the D4×S10 group; TTT a LoRA adapter (r=256, lr 5e-5, one epoch); decode 8 augmented views with threshold-DFS over grid tokens; rescore candidates under fresh views; aggregate by inverse consensus; select two distinct attempts with the coverage rule; write a schema-validated submission with exactly two attempts per input.  Sample shape 240 tasks / 259 inputs; 41,400 s budget; per-task cap 578 s if 240 tasks (1156 s if 120; hidden size unknown, 120–240).

Engineering: the packet pins the code source, runs offline, and forces deterministic fallback; a rerun-only `int("41400.0")` budget parse is now int-typed/defensive with a fault-injection gate. The CPU suite has 190 tests (~26 s); fallback 0/120 — a safety net.

## Selection study: a bounded negative result
We formalize the two-attempt decision as coverage optimization `U=1−P(¬C1∧¬C2)`: independent top-2 is order-free optimal, duplicates are dominated, and argmax-once is suboptimal under correlation. A calibrated plug-in uses p̂ and per-class ρ̂ behind a gate (Brier ≤0.10), with a pre-registered ≥+1.0-point test over top-2.

On real pools the theory does not convert into gains. CompressARC (400 tasks): oracle 34.25%, top-2 20.00%, argmax 19.50%, duplicate 18.50%, calibrated-independent 18.00%, coverage-optimal 17.75%, deployed 19.25% → `DOWNGRADE_TO_ANALYSIS` (margin −1.75 pp; 14.25-point oracle headroom, not reranking-reachable). B (16-input pilot): oracle and every deployable rule 31.25%, deployed 25.00%. C (simulated, mechanism only): oracle/coverage 41.67%, duplicate 16.67%, argmax 23.33%, top-2 21.67% (+18.33 pp, 95% CI [11.67,25.0]) — positive in simulation, never evidence about real solvers. The interpretation is score sufficiency: the solver's ordering already captures the recoverable two-attempt mass; the bottleneck is candidate coverage/generation, not selection.

## Theorem 4: the diversification threshold
For two distinct candidates with `p1 ≥ p2` and correctness correlation `ρ`, the pair beats duplicating the top-1 attempt iff `ρ < ρ* = √(p2(1−p1)/(p1(1−p2)))`; the gain is `√(p1(1−p1)p2(1−p2))·(ρ*−ρ)`. Equal marginals give `ΔU = p(1−p)(1−ρ)`; score sufficiency forces `ρ=1`, so no pair pays. The gate is derived, not tuned. Its empirical test on our GPU pools is EXPLORATORY: the protocol is frozen (`selection_protocol_v2.md` §6d), the tool `diversification_threshold.py` is self-tested, and the dev2 2-task run is labelled PILOT with no verdict; P1/P2 awaits the 10–20-task run after the 2026-10-24 quota refresh, with the falsifier stated in the paper appendix.

## New in v5: identifiability, not ranking, limits coverage
Distinct exact-match grids are mutually exclusive, so two attempts are a label-free identification problem. **Theorem 6** bounds any metadata rule: `E[U] ≤ p1+p2+min{T,√(I(C;Z)/2)}` (nats). On our pools the metadata term is non-binding (I=.059–.183 vs 2T²=.008–.041), so the unconditional certificate is **vacuous** — T6 is proven, not validated here. **Prop. 6e** states this non-vacuity condition; **Cor. 6g** records that full-metadata Pinsker bounds cannot certify label-free selection; **Prop. 6f** gives the constructive score-conditional bound `E[U] ≤ U_S+min{g−U_S,√(I(C;Z'|S)/2)}`, tight (equals `U_S`) under score sufficiency. Substantively, dev20 S2/S5/S5_ens recover the tail T exactly (ratio 1.00), while CompressARC loses −2.25 pp. **Theorem 7** (conditional on A1–A3) separates identification from compute: the unidentifiable gap is budget-invariant; the hidden-score ladder (16.25>15.42=15.42>14.17) is consistent, not conclusive (n=6; truncation; selection bug), and the candidate-prefix replay is definitional, not evidence.

## New in v6: verifier-induced identifiability (T8)
A bounded-DSL verifier (LegC T0–T2) accepted **0/120** public-eval tasks; 120/120 carry depth-2 impossibility certificates (35.05 CPU-min serial). Bounded-DSL verification certified **0/120** public-eval tasks (1/12 train control), so verifier-induced identifiability could not be realized with our bounded hypothesis class. F1a is vacuous, F1b/F3 are inconclusive/fail at depth 2; a 20-task depth-3 subset also certified 0/20 (not extrapolated to eval120), and F2's pre-registered verdict is FAIL (hybrid = TTT top-2, H−T=0); with zero verifier coverage the complementarity test is untestable and hybrid reduces exactly to TTT top-1+runner-up. The n=12 train control certifies **1/12** reachable task (0520fde7); learned-rule 0/120, chat 0/20, and reasoner 0/10 (token-truncated: infrastructure null) agree. Theorem 8 is conditional on reachability A0–A2; verifier-first beats TTT-first iff `E[1{U}(φ−p_w2)]>0`.

## Data characterization and leakage audit
Official competition files: 120 evaluation tasks / 172 test inputs (the repo mirror has 167; five tasks differ), 1,000 training tasks / 3,232 pairs / 1,076 inputs. A SHA-256 audit finds zero shared tasks, zero shared (input,output) pairs and zero shared input grids between training and evaluation. Evaluation is harder on every structural axis: mean maximum grid dimension 21.95 vs 14.67 (p<0.001), 64% of eval tasks in the hardest heuristic-proxy quartile, and eight single-rule heuristics solve 1.6% of training, 1.5% of holdout and 0% of public evaluation.

## Landscape and positioning
Public notebooks cluster at 29.7–31.4 displayed; their saved artifacts score only 1.67–4.17% task-level under official scoring (partial saved runs, not LB reruns). A concurrent instrumentation study of a faithful NVARC reproduction reports a comparable pattern (26.1% scored vs 32.5% perfect selection on 120 public-eval tasks) and a learned cross-validated reranker no better than the deployed vote rule; an unbiased pass@k diagnostic on ARC-AGI-1 finds selection already captures ≈95% of the candidate ceiling (generation-bound). The coverage objective is classical; our addition is the two-slot formalization, estimator and sufficiency criterion.

## Honest claims and limitations
No SOTA claim; no leaderboard claim; no hidden-test data used locally; no estimated score. Six scored hidden submissions span **14.17–16.25** versus **20.0%** on the 20-task public-eval subset — gaps consistent with truncation and subset noise; the earlier attempt errored (rerun-only budget parse), a CPU probe scored 0.00, and compute quota was exhausted Oct 31–Nov 1. There is no per-task hidden observability, so variance and failure attribution cannot be reported. Hidden-set size is unknown (120–240 tasks); runtime is queue time, not size evidence. Hidden-test results are one-shot and single-run; 16.25 remains far below the dated rank-percentile reference cutoffs, so no band claim is made; the ~30 public-notebook band is external context only. Selection is bounded by its third-party pools. Verifier numbers are public-eval diagnostics; labels scoring-only; 0/120 is a bounded-DSL impossibility certificate; LLM nulls infra-limited; train control n=12.
